**UNIQUENESS IS `specFace`, NEVER `specFingerprint`.** The face is shape + dimensions + mine count + sorted modifier set, what a player can tell apart. The fingerprint additionally separates `gimmickLevel`, `wallSegments` and the deduction caps, which measured on the old authored table reported 130 distinct specs over **109 distinct boards**: a ladder deduped on fingerprints still repeats 21 of them, which is what he hit at L65-70 ("I do not want a duplicate at all"). `gimmickLevel` is not even a stable property of a level, L63 (gl63) and L64 (gl64) both produce mirror intensity 2 across 300 draws, and post-intro dials take a random ±1 boost per draw. The 250-distinct-faces test carries its own non-vacuity check (a face must collapse the dials, and no single shape+modifier family may be big enough to have carried the ladder alone).

**REPRESENTATION IS A CONSTRAINT ON THE SEARCH, not a hope about its output** (his ruling: "I would like to see somewhat equal representation of gimmicks, tilings, etc. I do not want it to default to classic compass boards or something when there is a ton of space to explore here"). The shapes are two orders of magnitude apart on generation cost (4.8.8 ~21ms worst, rhombille ~2371ms), so one budget spent greedily measures whatever certifies cheapest, which is exactly what the old 39-entry pool showed (floret 10, deltoidal 9, against rect 1 and rhombille 1). The sweep is therefore ROUND-ROBIN over strata, one stratum = (shape × modifier set), every stratum getting the same number of VISITS whatever a visit costs it. Three ordering details are load-bearing and each was found by a run coming back lopsided: strata are interleaved SHAPE-MINOR (shape-major, the first smoke run measured 89 specs and every one was a rectangle); modifier sets are REPLICATED BY ARITY and interleaved (84 triples against 36 pairs, 9 singles and 1 plain would spend 65% of the budget on the arity the ladder uses least, and a 90-second run came back with 192 plain boards and none carrying mirror/sonar/compass/worm); and **the EMITTER round-robins (shape × modifier set) too**, because taking a shape's entries in arity order starves the modifiers, the low-price slices fill with plain boards, and all six shapes then debut in blocks 6-11 with the first modifier debut falling to block 24. Stratification the search does is thrown away if the last step does not preserve it.

**THE ASSIGNMENT** (`assignBraid` in challenge250.js) runs once at module load over 225 levels; it must be sequential because "nothing repeats" and "a thing joins the general draw after its debut" are both history-dependent. Each block asks the pool what it can carry at that difficulty over the same widening ladder the per-level pick walks (judging a debut on the strict band while picks may widen is what starved the first cut, introductions stalled and the assignment threw at L56). Shapes and modifiers ALTERNATE by how many of each has debuted, so they interleave instead of arriving in two runs. A debut runs exactly one block, so the lesson starts ON a checkpoint. When nothing new is viable AND the introduced material cannot fill the block, something is introduced anyway: the alternative is repeating a board. `MOD_INTRO_BLOCKS` and `SHAPE_INTRO_BLOCKS` are OUTPUTS now, not inputs, anything that pins a debut position must read them (test/worms.test.mjs and test/modifierCopy.test.mjs both had to). `UNINTRODUCED` reports what the pool could never place; empty is the contract, and a non-empty one is a POOL problem to fix by searching wider, never something to route around in the assignment.

Three parts: (1) `challengeSaveIsCurrent` (resumeEligibility) refuses any challenge save above `maxLevelReached + 1`, which is an INVARIANT of how the ladder advances (one level at a time, every win recorded, the selector only unlocking at or below that, `?level=` never persisting) rather than an epoch check, so it also covers every unstamped save already in the wild, works cross-device, and cannot refuse a real in-progress game; it gates both `isSaveResumable` and the checkpoint modal's Resume button, which used to read the slot raw. (2) `saveCheckpoint`/`loadCheckpoint` normalize through `getModeKey`, so `'normal'` and `'challenge'` are one number again. (3) A third migration marker, `challengeArtifactEpoch`, beside `challengeSeenEpoch` and for the same reason, drops the stale save and the orphaned legacy key, reaching players whose progression reset ALREADY ran. It runs AFTER the progression reset in the same call, because the position it judges a save against is the post-reset one. Pinned in `test/challenge250Reset.test.mjs` + the reworked `test/powerup-sync.test.mjs`.
