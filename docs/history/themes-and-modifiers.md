## Modifier (Gimmick) System
11 types defined in `src/logic/gimmicks.js`:
- walls (L11), liar (L21), mystery (L31), locked (L41), wormhole (L51), mirror (L61), pressurePlate (L71), sonar (L81), compass (L91), worm (L101), mineShift (chaos-only)
- 10-level intro blocks (intro to intro+9): primary gimmick always present
- Daily-safe subset: mystery, locked, walls, liar, wormhole, mirror, sonar, compass, worm (the bar is "board DATA static and canonical", worm's eggs are static cells; only its render-time overlay moves)
- **First-encounter cards are tracked BY REVISION, not by name** (his ruling 2026-08-04: "anytime a card is changed, it should be marked as not seen"). `minesweeper_seen_gimmicks` stores `{gimmick: revision}` where the revision is an FNV-1a hash DERIVED from the card's own player-visible content (name + desc + longDesc + exampleHtml); `minesweeper_seen_shapes` does the same over the shape card's title, copy and drawn patch, so a builder retune that changes the motif re-shows that card too. Editing a word therefore re-teaches the card on the next board that has it, with nothing to remember to bump, which is the whole point, since a declared version number is one somebody forgets during a copy edit. Why it matters: a player who learned the old sonar card ("a 5×5 area centered on the cell") has NOT seen the card that replaced it, and a correction has to reach exactly the people carrying the wrong idea. The legacy flat-array shape reads as UNSEEN once (an array entry genuinely does not record WHICH version was read) and migrates on the next mark.
- **The Challenge 250 epoch migration ALSO clears the modifier seen-set outright**, under its own `challengeSeenEpoch` marker. The ladder was rebuilt from level 1 for everyone, so walls at L6 and liar at L11 are first encounters again even for a returning player. The first cut of the reset deliberately left the set alone ("popups do NOT re-show") and produced exactly the report that killed that reasoning within a day of shipping: "I'm not getting popups on first wall or first liar." A SEPARATE marker is what reaches players whose progression reset already ran, folding it into the epoch guard would miss them, and bumping the epoch to catch them would wipe the climb they have built since.
- Popup can be disabled via `minesweeper_modifier_popup_disabled`
- **Persistent active-modifier reminder:** the `#active-gimmick-bar` (rendered above the board) shows icons for all currently-active modifiers in daily / weekly / challenge modes. Hidden when no modifiers are active or in chaos (which has its own bar, carrying the static "No guarantees" chip). The ✓ Certified chip does NOT live here, it rides the existing `#game-info-bar` row so gimmick-free boards (timed, most dailies) never pay an extra row for it on phones. `updateActiveGimmickBar()` in `src/ui/headerRenderer.js` updates the bar AND toggles the chip on `state.boardCertificate` (the same call sites settle both: end of `newGame()`, the post-generation stamp for challenge/timed, resume).
- **Liar visual cue:** liar cells use rose-pink background tint + italic + underlined number (CSS in `global.css` `.cell.liar-cell`). The pink background is the primary cue, italic+underline alone disappears at small mobile font sizes. Color avoids conflict with sonar cyan, compass gold, wormhole amber/magenta/green, and mirror blue/purple/green.
- **Per-cell stacking rules** (enforced in `applyGimmicks` via `hasBaseValueGimmick` / `hasDisplayBlockingGimmick`):
  - Base-value gimmicks (wormhole, mirror, sonar, compass) are mutually exclusive with each other, only one number can be displayed per cell.
  - Liar (±1 offset) stacks freely on any base-value gimmick. `recomputeDisplayedMines` computes the base value first, then applies the liar offset.
  - Locked stacks with any base-value gimmick and with liar, it's a temporary gate that reveals whatever the base/liar layers dictate once unlocked.
  - Mystery and pressure plate are fully exclusive (mystery masks the number, plate shows a timer instead of a count).
  - Worm eggs (`isWormEgg`) go only on PLAIN numbered safe cells, no mine, no other gimmick on the cell, so the post-hatch cell shows an ordinary number. `applyWorm` runs LAST in `ORDER` so its candidate filter sees every other gimmick already placed. Hidden eggs render as normal cells (a telegraphed safe-only egg would leak "this cell is safe").
- **Worm Tiles (L101, 2026-07-16):** revealing an egg hatches a worm (2-5 segments) that crawls one orthogonal step onto REVEALED cells at a SET per-worm cadence, `WORM_MOVE_BASE_MS` (1750ms, the old 0.5-3s roll's mean, so average speed and wormLoad are unchanged) × its PACE trait, a per-egg roll in [0.8, 1.3] (`WORM_PACE_MIN/MAX`), so a board has metronome-steady quick and slow worms (1.4s vs ~2.3s per move) that every player sees identically (his set-pace ruling 2026-08-03: "worms moving at a set pace, but their pace can vary as it has before", only the walk DIRECTION stays luck) (self-overlap allowed, spawns coiled on the egg, stays put when boxed in but still spends the move) and burrows after its OWN move budget, `wormLifetimeFor(seedIdentity, r, c)`, a per-EGG roll in [30, 80] (`WORM_LIFETIME_MIN/MAX_MOVES`) seeded like the length, so one daily worm can squat near-forever while another is a short cameo, yet every player sees the identical budgets (Christopher's ruling: per-worm variety, zero cross-player variability). Worms hatch at different times and each counts down alone, they never bury in sync. The step is NOT uniform, two layers: (1) MOMENTUM, with `WORM_PERSIST_PROB` (0.5) the worm first tries to continue its last heading when the cell ahead is walkable (`lastDir`, persisted; a pure random orthogonal walk backtracks so much its net travel grows like √moves, worms paced in place, Christopher's report); (2) otherwise candidates are roulette-weighted by `WORM_NUMBER_AVERSION^adjacentMines` (0.7, his "lightly weighted away from large numbers, toward lower ones"; a 0 beats a 4 about four-to-one, nothing is forbidden, and the worm reads TRUE counts, not liar-displayed values, a side effect is that big-number clue cells are covered less often), with the REVERSE of the heading additionally suppressed to `WORM_BACKTRACK_WEIGHT` (0.05) of its weight, ~5% reversal in a corridor, under 2% in open field, and a dead end still resolves by turning around since the tiny weight is then the whole roulette (his "5% or if it's stuck"). It's a memory tax, not a deduction mechanic: information is DELAYED, never destroyed, so **the solver is entirely worm-blind**, boardSolver never reads `isWormEgg`, certification/receipts are untouched, and that's the whole reason worm is daily-safe. **On a tiling the crawl is SIDE-ONLY (his ruling 2026-08-03, from the challenge-250 interview: "worms shouldn't cross through corners, only through sides like in the classic tiling")**: `buildWormCrawlTopology` (worms.js, the ONE builder timerManager's heartbeat calls) intersects the wireframe's side-sharing pairs with the live `_cellNeighbors`, the wireframe supplies geometry, the live adjacency supplies walls (a severed side stays uncrossable), so a Cubes rhombus offers 4 exits, not the corner-inclusive 10. A proven no-op on 4.8.8/honeycomb (trivalent: zero vertex-only pairs, crawl lists equal adjacency verbatim, test-pinned) and unreachable on rectangles (null topology keeps the dr/dc walk byte-identical). Side lists rebuild from `board._tiling` via the pure `buildTiling` (the renderer's own rebuild pattern; the crawl graph is mechanical, never certificational, so no stored-contract risk) and memoize per board. Pinned in `test/worms.test.mjs` with built-in controls: the lattice must HAVE corner links and the raw adjacency walk must CROSS one, or the gate fails as vacuous; break-tested (bypassing the side filter fails exactly the REGRESSION pin). Anatomy: pure logic in `src/logic/worms.js` (tunable constants; worm LENGTH is deterministic per egg via `createDailyRNG(seedIdentity:worm:r:c)` so every player on a canonical board hatches identical worms, only the walk is luck); hatch trigger is `hatchWormEggs` (timerManager) called from every player reveal batch (reveal cascade, chord, Reveal Safe) AFTER `revealWormholePairs`; the 250ms heartbeat lives beside the mine-shift interval and is torn down by `stopTimer` (which also clears worms, it only runs at true teardown), paused/resumed by `pauseTimer`/`resumeTimer`; segments + movesLeft persist in the auto-save and rehydrate with fresh clocks (`rehydrateWorms`, the plate re-arm precedent); rendering is `src/ui/wormRenderer.js`, a board-anchored sibling overlay (walls pattern) at z-index 4 with element reuse so the CSS left/top transition tweens the crawl, segments center on the cell's VISUAL center via `wormCellCenterUnitOffsets` (worms.js: cellPos minus the polygon bbox center, in pitch units, zero on every centrally symmetric cell, ~0.25P on a floret pentagon and ~0.21P on a deltoidal kite, the same asymmetry `_anchorPadding` corrects for the number; without it the pitch-sized segment centered on the BOX read off-center and oversized on petals/kites, his report 2026-08-03), `pointer-events: none` (covered cells stay clickable/chordable, no special interaction rules), repositioned on worm ticks + window resize + theme refit; each worm carries a seeded per-egg TONE (0..1, `wormToneFor`, cosmetic only, persisted with the worm) that the renderer interpolates between the theme's `--worm-tone-dark`/`--worm-tone-light` endpoint tokens (brown→cream on the base design; shading self-derives from the mixed base via `SHADE_ANCHOR`), mixed in JS (`mixHex`, node-tested) NOT CSS color-mix, a var-dependent color-mix failing on an older engine computes to no background at all, an invisible worm. A theme recolors its whole brood by overriding just the two endpoints (Christopher: theme-based worm families later). Worm is the first **sprite-only modifier**: no `icon` field in its def, no emoji anywhere, every icon surface renders `assets/sprites/mod-worm.svg` (`modWorm` has no `defaultEmoji`; the gimmick sprite chain resolves by key) and every `def.icon` consumer carries a missing-icon guard (the text share line just omits it). Par feature **`wormLoad`** = Σ per-egg (length × lifetime × pace) / `WORM_LOAD_SCALE` (100), pace belongs in the exposure measure because a slow worm covers numbers longer per move and lives longer in wall-clock. That is the SCHEDULED (maximum) dose; the FIT uses each row's REALIZED dose instead: every hatch is logged to `state.wormEvents` (`{t, r, c, len, life, pace, moves, tEnd?}`, traits embedded so R never re-derives the seeded RNG; `moves` = exact realized count, stamped `life` at burrow and `life − movesLeft` at stopTimer's finalize for worms alive at game end), submitted with daily/weekly/archive scores exactly as bombHitEvents are (rules-whitelisted per-field in both row families, pinned in `test/rules-contract.test.mjs`; extras contract pinned in `test/winSubmissionPlan.test.mjs`; persisted/restored with the save). Fitting on the schedule would ATTENUATE the coefficient, fast solvers and late hatches realize less of it, systematically. The refit computes `wormRealized = Σ len × moves / 100` per row (falling back to scheduled for old-client rows without events) and ships `secPerWormLoad` bridged by the play-weighted realization ratio, since predictPar can only know the schedule pre-play, pre-programmed segment-moves in hundreds (~0.6-12, the count features' numeric range), fully structural (derives from egg positions + the payload's `rngSeed`, which every recompute site now threads into `computeDailyFeatures`, client state carries `dailyRngSeed`/`weeklyRngSeed`, canonical recomputes pass `rngSeed`), in the verify sweep's hard-fail keys; egg COUNT alone can't carry the burden once lifetime varies 30-80. The R refit gates the `wormLoad` term on real data existing (`add_worm_term`, the archivePlay zero-variance pattern) AND zero-guards the shipped `secPerWormLoad` below 20 nonzero plays. Tests: `test/worms.test.mjs`.
- **Displayed-number source of truth:** `recomputeDisplayedMines(board)` in `gimmicks.js` is the single function that writes `displayedMines`. Called at the end of `applyGimmicks` and after any mine-layout change (`defuseMine`, `shieldDefuse`, `magnetPull`, `performMineShift`).
- **Adjacency source of truth:** `src/logic/adjacency.js` owns the whole question of what touches what, `wallKey`, `hasWallBetween`, `buildNeighborCache`, `defineCellNeighbors` (see the Board Topology section). It is a LEAF module importing nothing, which is what lets `gimmicks.js` and `boardSolver.js` both depend on it; the cycle between those two is why the primitive used to be split across them. `countAdjacentMines(board, r, c, neighborCache?)` (per-cell) and `recalcAllAdjacency(board)` (whole board) in `gimmicks.js` are the ONE mine counter and now read their neighbors from `buildNeighborCache` rather than re-deriving them; `boardGenerator` and `powerUps` call them rather than re-implementing the loop. **Pass a cache to `countAdjacentMines` from any loop**, bare, it derives the whole board's neighbor lists per call, turning an O(cells) sweep into O(cells²). **The convention: a mine carries no number, so its `adjacentMines` is ALWAYS 0.** Four copies used to exist and they disagreed on exactly that branch, `calculateAdjacency` (now deleted) SKIPPED mine cells, so a cell `swapMines` promoted from safe to mine kept the count it held while safe; that stale value serialized into a canonical and the nightly verify sweep flagged it (`dailyBoard/2026-07-16`, 2026-07-10). The deliberate scoped exceptions (each honoring the mine=0 convention) are `boardGenerator`'s incremental `placeMineIncremental` / `removeMineIncremental` (O(8) `neighborCache` deltas) and `cruxExtract`'s teaser mini-board recompute; the sonar radius-2 / compass ray / wormhole-sum scans in `recomputeDisplayedMines` are different geometry, not this counter. Pinned by `test/adjacency.test.mjs`.
- **Mirror is a 2-cell adjacent swap** (not a 2x2 zone). Pair count scales 1-3 with intensity. Cells store `mirrorPair = { row, col, pairIndex }`.
- **Walls:** `applyWalls` ALWAYS calls `recalcAllAdjacency` at the end, even when its isolation check forces walls to be cleared. Without this, cells retain stale wall-aware counts from a prior pass (shown-too-low vs. actual mines). `applyGimmicks` skips re-rolling walls when `board._wallEdges` is already populated (challenge mode pre-applies walls via `preWallEdges` so the constructive generator can build a wall-aware layout; re-rolling would invalidate it).
- **Wall rendering:** `renderWallOverlays()` is called at the end of `newGame()` so daily mode's pre-generated walls are visible before first click. Challenge and chaos modes also call it inside their first-click handler (gimmicks apply on first click in those modes). Wall lines are pixel-anchored to live cell rects (like the worm overlay), so any path that moves the cells must re-render them: the window-resize handler and `applyThemeLive`'s post-CSS refit both call `renderWallOverlays()` (idempotent, drops and rebuilds the overlay container). Pinned by `tests/e2e/wallResize.spec.mjs`.


## Theme System
- **26 kept themes** unlocked by level progression (trimmed from 50 in 2026-06: every kept theme carries the full per-theme kit, objects + contrasting grout + confetti palette + reveal choreography; the 24 cut themes live in git history and restoring one means restoring its CSS + registry entries and bringing it up to the contract). Count is FROZEN, the feel budget goes into deepening these, not adding palettes.
- **Unlock ladder rule (re-spread for Challenge 250):** classic + dark free at level 0; the other 24 themes unlock at the opener capstone **L25** (clearing the openers, the door to the shapes), then **one every 10 levels from L30 to L250**, so Legendary lands on the crown. Every unlock sits ON a checkpoint, or a player could pass it mid-block and miss the reward moment. **Themes fully relock at the C250 progression reset** (unlocks derive from maxLevelReached, which the reset zeroes, nothing per-theme is stored). Order unchanged, by IN-YOUR-FACE-NESS (quiet print-and-paper worlds first; loud, animated, high-saturation worlds as late-game rewards). `test/themeUnlockLadder.test.mjs` enforces the structure: L25 then every 10th to `CHALLENGE_MAX_LEVEL`, no gaps or doubles, every level a checkpoint multiple, the last theme on the crown, registry listed in unlock order (the Collection grid renders in registry order). Restoring a cut theme means giving it a ladder slot, which bumps everything after it.
- CSS custom properties per theme in `src/styles/themes/`
- `THEME_UNLOCKS` in `src/ui/themeManager.js` maps theme to required level. The boot path guards saved themes that are locked OR no longer exist (cut themes fall back to the highest unlocked).
- Num colors 9-20 exist for wormhole sums (9-18 for the rectangular 8+8 range; 19-20 added 2026-08-01 for tiling sums, a rhombille pair of valence-10 cells reaches 20, the ceiling `test/wormholeSumTokens.test.mjs` computes from the shipped lattices); dark themes override them with bright variants
- `--color-wall` contrast must be >= 3.5:1 against BOTH cell backgrounds (revealed AND hidden). For themes whose two backgrounds sit at opposite luminance extremes (light panes + dark fog, stained glass, apothecary, split-flap), no single color can satisfy both, so `.wall-line` supports a two-tone wall: the `--color-wall` body covers one side and `--color-wall-outline` (a 1.5px box-shadow ring, default transparent) covers the other. The floor is then: each background must be cleared by body OR outline.
- **Audio palettes**: `THEME_SOUND_PALETTES` in `src/audio/sounds.js` re-voices the four core moment sounds (reveal, flag, cascade, win) per theme. ALL 25 non-classic themes ship a voice (2026-06-10); `classic` is deliberately paletteless, the default synth IS its identity. Voice-design rules are in the file header (distinctness = timbre x register x direction; saw moments set the optional `cascade.vol`/`win.vol`; cascades may descend but `base + 7*step` must stay audible). `test/themeSoundPalettes.test.mjs` enforces shape, loudness ceilings, key integrity against THEME_UNLOCKS (a mis-keyed palette silently never fires), and a pairwise distinctness gate, new palettes must claim an unoccupied corner of the voice space.
- **Receipt-moment tokens** (`--receipt-pulse-color`, `--receipt-verdict-bg/-border`, `--receipt-crux-outline`) are reserved in `:root` so the deduction-receipt surfaces land themed per world when they ship.

### Theme Expression Tokens (the "wildly different per theme" layer)
The `:root` block in `global.css` exposes a large token contract so a theme can diverge in **motion, type, texture, cell treatment, and feedback colors**, not just palette. Every token defaults byte-for-byte to the value that used to be hardcoded, so a theme that overrides nothing renders exactly as before. Groups:
- **Motion:** `--anim-reveal-dur/-ease`, `--anim-flag-dur/-ease`, `--anim-mine-dur/-ease`, `--ease-bounce`, `--ease-standard`. Only the `animation` shorthand (duration/easing) is tokenized, `@keyframes` interiors can't read vars, so a theme changes *choreography* by shipping its own keyframes in its theme file. History note: the flag/mine tokens were declared but DEAD (`.cell.flag-pop` / `.cell.mine-chain` hardcoded their literals) until 2026-06-10; the root defaults were corrected to the effective literals (flag 0.35s, mine 0.4s) when wired, so tokenless themes render byte-identically while the ~17 themes that had set flag tokens finally animate as designed. Flag choreography overrides use `[data-theme=X] .cell.flag-pop { animation-name: ... }`.
- **Type scale:** `--fs-title`, `--fs-lcd`, `--fs-cell-number`, `--fs-mine`, `--fs-flag`.
- **Cell + sizing:** `--cell-shape-radius`; `--cell-min-size` / `--cell-max-size` / `--cell-fit-max-size` are read by JS in `boardRenderer.js` (`resizeCells`/`adjustCellSize`) because JS owns `--cell-size` at runtime, a CSS `--cell-size` override would be clobbered, so these bound tokens are the only way a theme can widen/tighten cells. `--grid-gap` also participates in board width (candy 3px, matrix 1px vs the 2px default), so BOTH fitters read the live computed gap and `applyThemeLive` re-runs `resizeCells` after the new theme's lazy stylesheet loads (`loadThemeCSS` returns a promise resolving on link.onload), without that refit a wider gap pushed the fit-content board past its container (cells spilling off the side, fixed 2026-07-12; pinned by `tests/e2e/themeCellFit.spec.mjs`). `--cell-gap-seal` = the grout color painted between cleared cells when an effect is on (see Effect layering). It defaults to `--color-cell-revealed`, but **that default is the banned "slab" look (grout == tile) and must NEVER ship on an effect-bearing theme**, see the grout-contrast HARD rule below. Every theme that registers a `THEME_EFFECTS` entry sets its own contrasting grout (forest sage, candy pink, circuit-board copper trace, nest twig weave, inferno lava crack, stained-glass dark lead, etc.).
- **Backgrounds:** `--bg-page-image` (a fixed `body::before` layer, default `none`), `--bg-page-blend`, `--bg-board` (`#board-container`). Vector only (gradients / CSS patterns / SVG data-URIs), no raster assets, so no `sw.js` ASSETS/CACHE_NAME coupling. Animated / board ambiance routes through `src/ui/themeEffects.js`, not CSS. **Effect layering:** the `.theme-fx` container is `z-index: 1`, between unrevealed cells (z-index auto/0) and revealed cells (`.cell.revealed` is `z-index: 2`), so ambient animations (rain, leaves, sparks, stars) play over the fog + background gaps but never wash over revealed numbers/markers. Walls stay on top at `z-index: 3`. Each theme's effect should keep particle counts modest but the particles large/visible (the original Forest spores were 2-4 sub-5px fading dots = invisible; falling leaves at 13-22px read clearly). **Gap-seal occlusion:** lifting revealed cells above the effect handles the cell *bodies*, but the 1-2px inter-cell *gaps* would still show the effect as slivers. `applyThemeEffects` adds `.fx-on` to `#board` only when a particle effect is registered (effect-less themes are byte-identical, no seal, no z-index lift). **Software-rendering gate (2026-06-17):** `applyThemeEffects` early-returns (treating every effect theme as effect-less, no `.fx-on`) under `prefers-reduced-motion` OR software compositing, `isSoftwareRendering()` in `src/logic/deviceCapability.js` probes the WebGL `UNMASKED_RENDERER` and matches SwiftShader / Microsoft Basic Render Driver / llvmpipe / software (or no GL context at all). Per-frame DOM particles are cheap on a GPU but stutter the whole game on the CPU, so a software-compositing browser (integrated/VM/remote, or a GPU-driver fallback that can hit even a strong card) keeps the theme's colors/sprites/static backdrop but loses the live particles. The pure string matcher `isSoftwareRenderer(str)` is node-tested in `test/softwareRender.test.mjs`; an unknown/blocked renderer string is conservatively treated as NOT software (real GPU with the debug ext hidden for privacy must keep its effects). As of wave 2 (2026-06-10), 24 of 26 themes register an effect; only classic and dark stay deliberately bare (restraint is their identity, like classic's default synth voice). Wave 2 effects (editorial ink flecks + headline rules, sumi-e ink motes + brush wisps, blueprint drafting ticks + measure lines, cartography route dashes + sounding rings, origami gliding paper triangles, nest gulls gliding across the sky) are VECTOR-DRAWN, styled divs with gradients / clip-paths / dash patterns, never emoji glyph particles, per the "worlds apart, less emoji" direction; prefer that style for any future effect work. Under `.fx-on`, each revealed cell gets a two-layer seal: a `::before` SQUARE solid fill of `--cell-gap-seal` over cell+gap (z-index -2; square so four neighbors tile every 4-cell junction shut, a rounded ring or box-shadow leaves a diamond there, the inherent weak spot of rounded corners), and a `::after` rounded `--color-cell-revealed` body on top (z-index -1) so each tile shows its own surface and the grout reads only in the gaps. The seal EXCLUDES the face-cue markers, sonar/compass/wormhole (translucent bg tints), locked/pressure-plate/defused (`::after` glyphs), and MIRROR (dashed border): the fx-on z-lift makes each revealed cell a stacking context, and negative-z pseudos paint ABOVE the element's own background and borders, so a sealed mirror cell's dashes were buried (found 2026-06-11, the mirror daily on chalkboard, invisible pairs in prod). The tint markers get an opaque revealed base composited under their tint on `.fx-on` so the effect can't bleed through them; a mirror PAIR is adjacent, so the seam between the two excluded cells can show a 1px effect sliver (accepted). Liar (outline, outlines paint last) and mystery (text) survive the body layer and keep the full seal. Colorblind mode (whose `::after` is a shape glyph) forces `--cell-gap-seal` back to the revealed color so the tile reads as one cleared surface. Verified by dyeing the effect a unique color and pixel-sampling every gap + junction: 0 leak. **Holistic fog art (2026-06-11):** `#board` consumes `--bg-fog-art` (default `none`) as a background-image sized 100% x 100% under the cells; a theme sets it to ONE large vector drawing (data-URI SVG or gradient) and switches its `.cell.unrevealed` gradient + `--color-cell-hidden-hover` to TRANSLUCENT rgba tints, so the drawing reads through the fog as one continuous image spanning the board (revealed cells stay opaque -- revealing tears the sheet). Compositing base is `#board`'s background-color (`--color-border`); the gallery mini-boards mirror that. Seven worlds use it: origami (crane crease pattern), cartography (worn chart), nest (open sky, clouds + a corner sun), blueprint (cyanotype sheet + white number set), chalkboard (half-erased workings + chalk-dust number text-shadow), noir (venetian-blind bands), ocean (sounding chart). Fog must STILL read as interactive tiles: keep borders/hover and tint alphas around 0.3-0.55, and drawings need bolder strokes than instinct suggests (the fog tint sits on top).
- **Special-cell markers:** `--marker-liar-outline(-width)`, `--marker-sonar-tint`, `--marker-compass-tint`, `--marker-wormhole-0/1/2`, `--marker-mirror-0/1/2(-width)`, `--marker-plate-timer/-border/-glow`. See legibility contract below.
- **Overlays / power-ups / timer / victory:** `--overlay-*` (wrong/correct flag, suggest move/start, mine-hit glow), `--pu-*` (shield, xray, lifeline, magnet), `--timer-warning/-critical/-critical-glow`, `--victory-color/-duration`.

**Special-cell legibility contract (HARD FLOOR, do not relax):** marker tokens may be re-hued slightly by a theme only to avoid clashing with its own background. Every marker must stay (a) unmistakable as its modifier, (b) mutually distinct, (c) high-contrast vs the cell bg (line markers ≥ 3:1, like `--color-wall`), (d) above the alpha floors (tints ≥ 0.12, borders ≥ 0.6). Identity is anchored in a non-color channel (📡/arrow glyph, italic+underline, dashed border vs outline vs fill) so meaning never rests on hue alone. The markers keep their `!important` so an aggressive cell-bg can't bury them.

**Grout-contrast contract (HARD RULE, absolute, do not relax):** for any theme that registers a `THEME_EFFECTS` entry (so `.fx-on` is set and the gap-seal is painted), `--cell-gap-seal` MUST be a color that visibly CONTRASTS `--color-cell-revealed`, never equal to it. Grout == tile produces a solid "slab" where cleared cells merge into one undifferentiated block; this was explicitly rejected ("I see that we might be making grout fill that's the same as the tiles. Absolutely don't do that."). Cleared cells must always read as separate tiles set in a contrasting grout (chalk line on slate, copper trace on PCB, twig weave between nest tiles, dark lead between stained-glass panes, glowing lava crack between charred inferno tiles). Practical floor: luminance delta ≥ ~50 between grout and tile, and the grout should be thematically motivated (it's the "what's between the tiles" of the world), not an arbitrary outline. The default (`var(--color-cell-revealed)`) is intentionally the slab so an *effect-less* theme stays byte-identical, but it is a bug on any effect theme. Verify by revealing a region and confirming the gap lines are clearly a different color from the tile bodies. Colorblind mode is the one exception: it forces gap-seal back to the revealed color (the `::after` shape glyph carries identity instead).

**Per-theme objects + moments contract:** every theme defines its game objects and key moments in its own idiom, not just palette. (a) **Objects** live in `THEME_UNLOCKS` (`themeManager.js`) as emoji fields: `mine`, `flag`, `strikeCell` (the detonated cell, falls back to `mine` then `💣` if unset), plus `smiley`/`smileyWin`/`smileyLoss`. `getThemeEmoji(type)` in `boardRenderer.js` resolves them (theme-owned only, emoji packs, the old per-player override layer, were cut with the 2026-06-12 Collection declutter along with the effects and titles tabs; `collectionManager.js` is deleted and Collection is themes-only). Architected so hand-drawn SVGs swap in later via the same classes with zero JS rewiring. That swap is now live as **`THEME_SPRITES` in `src/ui/spriteLoader.js`** (theme -> {mine, flag, strikeCell} -> `assets/sprites/themes/<theme>-<object>.svg`): `getSpriteUrl` consults it before the Tier 1 canonical-emoji match, and a theme sprite renders ONLY when the resolved emoji equals that theme's own object emoji (always true now; kept as the identity contract between getThemeEmoji and THEME_UNLOCKS) -- undrawn themes keep their emoji verbatim. Batch 1 (2026-06-11) ships editorial / sumie / blueprint / cartography / chalkboard / splitflap / galaxy / circuitboard / nest (the egg / omelette / feather set, replacing the retired comic 2026-06-25) full sets plus noir's strikeCell (the crime-scene chalk body outline). All 28 files are in the sw.js ASSETS precache. Ship future worlds by adding files + one registry line. (b) **Reveal moment:** each theme overrides `.cell.num-pop, .cell.revealing { animation-name: <theme>Reveal }` with its own `@keyframes` in its theme file (editorialPrint, sumieBleed, candyPop, chalkScrawl, noirFlicker, glassBloom, flapFlip, circuitPower, nestHatch, …). (c) **Win moment:** per-theme confetti palette in `effectsRenderer.js` (the `themeColors` map inside `showConfettiBurst`, keyed by `state.theme`, beware: a palette under a stale key silently never fires; sakura's sat under its pre-rename key 'cherry-blossom' until 2026-06). (d) **Explosion moment:** `showExplosionBurst(originX, originY, count)` in `effectsRenderer.js` fires a radial, theme-colored detonation at the hit cell from `chainRevealMines` (challenge/timed loss). It reuses `showConfettiBurst`'s particle engine via physics `opts` (the confetti defaults are unchanged). A new theme must supply, at minimum, its objects + a contrasting grout (if it has an effect) + a confetti palette entry.

**Regression invariant + how to verify:** because every default equals the old literal, all themes are byte-identical until one deliberately overrides a token. The frozen computed-style probe is a COMMITTED TOOL: `scripts/theme-style-probe.html` (serve the repo with `python -m http.server 8080`, open the page, it hashes synthetic marker/overlay/timer/cell elements across all 26 themes; capture `window.__probeResults` on the BASELINE checkout, then paste into the changed checkout's page and Diff). Implementation notes baked into the tool: animations are frozen via `animation-play-state: paused` at delay 0, NOT `animation: none`, which would erase `animation-name`/`duration` from computed styles and blind the probe to choreography identity; stylesheet links are cache-busted per run (a same-browser baseline would otherwise diff cached CSS against itself and report "no differences" for real changes); `.num-pop`/`.revealing` probe elements carry reveal-choreography identity; keyframe INTERIORS remain by-eye review. Workflow for a theme wave: `git stash push` the theme edits → baseline → `git stash pop` → diff; only deliberately redesigned themes may appear.
