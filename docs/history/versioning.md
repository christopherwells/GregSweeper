## Version
Current: **v1.10** (`CURRENT_VERSION` in `main.js`, drives the What's New NEW badge), cache `gregsweeper-v1.10.N` in `sw.js` where N is the live per-deploy counter (read it from `sw.js`; it moves on nearly every PR, so a number written here goes stale within a day and is deliberately not pinned). The two are kept in lockstep on `MAJOR.MINOR`, see the versioning rule under Deploy. **v1.10 is the Coastline + Challenge 250 release (2026-08-04)**: the daily shape rotation flipped live and the 250-level ladder replaced the sawtooth, bundled as ONE release because they are one arc and because the ladder is what walks a player through all seven shapes. **The weekly card additions (week streak, past weeklies, par) shipped on the `N` counter with NO What's New entry at all, his two rulings, 2026-08-05, each correcting the last: "this is not worth a whole .1 bump, these are really small increments", then "these aren't what's new worthy either."** The work is player-facing, which is the bar as written for a MINOR, and being player-facing turned out to be necessary and nowhere near sufficient, for either surface.
  - **The MINOR bar is the size of a RELEASE**, and v1.10 set that unit at six tilings plus a 250-level ladder. Three additions to one card is a different class of thing. Ask **would this headline a release next to the last one?** before reaching for a bump.
  - **WHAT'S NEW IS NOT A CHANGELOG.** That is the mistake underneath both rulings. Its entry is attached to a badge that interrupts a player, so the bar is not "did something change" but "is this worth stopping them for", and a card that explains itself the moment they look at it is not. The Past chip is visible, the streak and par read as what they are, so nothing needed announcing. Work of this kind ships silent: it lands on `N`, it goes in CLAUDE.md, and the player finds it where it lives.
  - Rolling a shipped bump back is cheap but not free: `CURRENT_VERSION` drives the NEW badge off `lastSeenVersion` inequality, so anyone who had already opened What's New under the withdrawn version sees the badge once more. v1.10 is also the standing example of the rule working in the other direction: six tilings, the per-shape par model, the Par Lab and the par bands all landed on the `N` counter first, because none of them was a thing a player could feel until this commit made them reachable.
